📖 Islamic Finance Knowledge Base

Understand the principles of Islamic finance and how they apply to your life.

📜

What is Zakat?

Zakat is the third pillar of Islam — an obligatory annual charity of 2.5% on wealth held for one lunar year above the Nisab threshold. It purifies wealth and supports those in need.

💝

Sadaqah vs Zakat

Sadaqah is voluntary charity given at any time in any amount. While Zakat is obligatory with fixed rules, Sadaqah is recommended daily and can be as simple as a smile.

⚠️

Riba (Interest)

Islam strictly prohibits Riba (usury/interest). All financial transactions must be free from interest, promoting ethical and fair economic relationships.

⚖️

Nisab Explained

Nisab is the minimum amount of wealth a Muslim must possess before Zakat becomes obligatory. It's equivalent to the value of 85 grams of gold or 595 grams of silver.

🏦

Islamic Banking

Sharia-compliant banking operates on profit-sharing (Mudarabah), partnership (Musharakah), and asset-backed financing — ensuring ethical investments.

📊

Who Receives Zakat?

The Quran specifies eight categories: the poor, needy, Zakat collectors, those whose hearts are to be reconciled, slaves, debtors, in the cause of Allah, and the wayfarer.
